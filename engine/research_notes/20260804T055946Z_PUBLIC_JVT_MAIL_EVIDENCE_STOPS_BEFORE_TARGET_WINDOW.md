## Evidence ledger

### 1. Direct facts

#### F1 - The 2003 JVT reflector was external to the current ITU archive

S1 identifies the general discussion system as an IMTC-hosted Lyris reflector
with address `jvt-experts@mail.imtc.org`. The same page separately identifies
the IMTC FTP as the document site. S2 later names an RWTH reflector and again
lists the ITU JVT document archive separately.

This directly supports a service distinction. It does not prove that IMTC or
RWTH never exported messages into a private or unindexed archive.

#### F2 - The current public ITU JVT landing page is a meeting-document tree

S2's landing page exposes dated meeting directories and standardization files.
It does not expose a browseable `jvt-experts` mail tree. The previous run already
searched the relevant 2003 meeting directories and found the formal documents,
not email messages.

Boundary: absence from the landing page is not a global nonexistence claim.
An unlinked, access-controlled, renamed, or separately hosted mail archive may
still exist.

#### F3 - At least one participant's reflector mail was preserved independently

S3's official exhibit 2441 contains a dated message from Karsten Sühring and an
explicit reference to reflector subscription. The related public exhibit list
enumerates hundreds of individually titled email records. Thus reflector mail
survived outside the current public JVT meeting-document tree.

#### F4 - The located litigation selection stops before the target window

The S3 exhibit list begins with a 2 May 2002 reflector message and its last
listed email records are dated 24 December 2002. The list contains no `2003-`
dated email item and no `film grain` item. It therefore cannot answer the narrow
10 April-31 July 2003 H022/I013 question.

Boundary: this is the scope of the exhibits selected for IPR2024-00572, not the
scope of Sühring's entire mailbox or of any discovery production.

#### F5 - The obvious public Technicolor archive is chronologically irrelevant

S4 states that its corporate-archive selection spans 1916-1986. A 2003 Thomson
Princeton project falls outside that scope by seventeen years. Searching it for
the H022 edit history would conflate two different corporate/archive contexts.

#### F6 - InterDigital is a defensible successor lead but not yet a record source

S5 documents the 2018 transfer of Technicolor's patent-licensing business,
video-coding patent assets, and personnel to InterDigital. It does not document
transfer or availability of project correspondence. No public InterDigital
finding aid for H022, `PU030116`, the Lyris list, or the named authors was found
in this run.

### 2. Bounded inferences

#### I1 - Repeating the current ITU meeting-directory search is low value

Reasoning chain:

1. S1 separates the mail service from the document FTP.
2. S2 preserves that distinction in later official terminology.
3. The target meeting directories were already exhaustively checked in the
   preceding run.
4. Therefore further searches of the same directory family are unlikely to
   recover correspondence unless a new filename or external pointer appears.

This does not lower the value of a newly discovered IMTC/RWTH export.

#### I2 - Litigation and participant-held mailboxes are now stronger archive routes

S3 proves that participant-held reflector email can become independently public
through litigation even when no mail archive is visible beside the JVT document
tree. Searching later P-TACTS/ITC exhibit sets, declarations, and discovery
indexes for 2003 dates is therefore better grounded than another broad web
search for a generic `JVT archive`.

Boundary: the currently found IPR selection is specifically about parameter-set
software work and ends in 2002; it is not evidence that film-grain correspondence
was produced.

#### I3 - Corporate-successor outreach must ask about records, not just patents

S5 makes InterDigital a plausible organizational lead, but patent ownership does
not imply custody of every 2003 email or source file. A future archival inquiry
must name the exact records sought and ask separately about engineering records,
standards-contribution drafts, Lyris exports, and inventor files.

## Conclusion

### Confirmed

- JVT's general reflector in the relevant era was an IMTC-hosted Lyris service,
  separate from its public document FTP.
- The present ITU JVT landing page exposes meeting documents, not a visible
  historic reflector-message tree.
- Genuine participant-held JVT reflector mail survives in public USPTO exhibits.
- The specific public IPR2024-00572 email selection located here covers 2 May-24
  December 2002 and does not reach the April-July 2003 target interval.
- The public Technicolor Online Research Archive's stated 1916-1986 scope does
  not cover the project.
- InterDigital's 2018 acquisition supplies a plausible successor lead, but no
  public finding aid establishes custody of the needed correspondence.

### Denied

- The located USPTO exhibit set does not explain the H022 row edit or I013 field
  removal.
- The current ITU document directory should not be represented as a complete
  mail archive.
- Endpoint failure and lack of indexed hits do not prove destruction or
  nonexistence of the messages.
- The historical Technicolor archive is not an appropriate substitute for
  Thomson's 2003 Princeton engineering records.

### Still unknown

- Whether a public or private 2003 reflector export exists.
- Whether any participant or corporate custodian retained the decisive messages
  or pre-H022 draft.
- Why 5279 became 5218 and why stock identification disappeared from public
  I013.
- Every missing 5279-specific physical grain parameter remains missing.
